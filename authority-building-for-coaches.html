Authority building for coaches: from another coach to the coach

16 September 2025 · Rogue Coach Teams

Introduction

The coaching industry has exploded in the last decade. With low barriers to entry, more people than ever are stepping into the coaching world career coaches, health coaches, business coaches, mindset coaches, spiritual coaches, and the list goes on. This is both exciting and overwhelming. Exciting, because more people now have access to life-changing coaching. Overwhelming, because it’s harder than ever for you a serious, dedicated coach to stand out from the noise.

Here’s the truth: being a good coach isn’t enough anymore. Clients don’t just hire coaches based on skills or certifications they hire the ones they trust. And trust comes from one thing: authority.

What Is Authority (And Why Does It Matter for Coaches)?

Authority is the invisible force that makes people choose you instead of scrolling past or shopping around for cheaper alternatives. When you have authority:

  • You no longer chase clients clients chase you.
  • People perceive you as credible before they’ve even spoken to you.
  • Opportunities like podcasts, partnerships, and speaking gigs naturally flow your way.
  • You can charge premium rates because you’ve positioned yourself as the expert.

Authority is not about the loudest voice, the biggest following, or flashy funnels. It’s a reputation that signals: this coach knows their subject, has proof, and can be trusted to deliver results.

Why Most Coaches Struggle With Authority

Despite talent, many coaches remain stuck in the invisible zone. Common reasons include:

  • Copy-Paste Marketing: Recycling the same quotes and templates that make you indistinguishable.
  • Over-Selling, Under-Proving: Making big promises without credibility markers like case studies or features.
  • Lack of Assets: No books, PR, or other authority assets so reliance remains on ads, DMs, and referrals.

The result is a cycle of hustling, undercharging, and burnout. The good news: there are proven ways out.

The 3 Pillars of Authority Building

1. Positioning: Define Your Unique Expertise

You can’t be “a coach for everyone.” Authority comes from clarity. Who do you help? How do you help them? Why should they listen to you?

Instead of: I’m a life coach. Try: I help mid-career women break through self-doubt and land leadership roles within 6 months.

2. Visibility: Show Up Where It Matters

Be present in spaces where your audience searches for solutions. This isn’t posting for the sake of consistency it’s strategic visibility.

  • Host niche webinars
  • Guest on relevant podcasts
  • Speak at industry events
  • Publish long-form thought leadership

3. Credibility: Prove You’re the Real Deal

Real authority needs markers. Examples include case studies, testimonials, media features, JV collaborations, and a published book.

The Authority Shortcut: Becoming an Author

Most thought leaders you admire have written a book. Why? Because a book instantly sets you apart and signals deep mastery.

What a book does for you

  • Positioning: Defines your philosophy and method.
  • Credibility: “Published author” upgrades your bio.
  • Visibility: Opens PR, speaking, and JV doors.
  • Pipeline: Acts as a lead magnet that warms clients before discovery calls.

Why a book is an authority engine

A book does the heavy lifting: it explains your value, showcases outcomes, and gives prospects a low-friction way to trust you.

Why Most Coaches Don’t Write Books (And Why They Should)

Objections keep good coaches from publishing:

  • “I don’t have the time.”
  • “I’m not a writer.”
  • “I don’t know how to publish or market it.”

The reality: you don’t have to do it alone. With the right process and support, a book is one of the fastest ways to break out of the invisible zone.
